Absolute value|The distance of a number from zero, ignoring its sign.
Adapted|A process is adapted when its value at each date uses only the information available at that date.
Algebra|The use of symbols and equality-preserving operations to describe and solve relationships.
Antiderivative|A function whose derivative equals a specified function. Antiderivatives on an interval differ by an additive constant.
Arbitrage|A trading opportunity with no initial net cost, no possible loss and a positive chance of gain, under a specified market model.
Autocorrelation|The correlation of a series with a lagged version of itself.
Basis|A set of independent vectors whose linear combinations span the space being described.
Basis point|One hundredth of a percentage point, or 0.0001 as a decimal rate.
Bayesian|An approach that combines a prior distribution with a likelihood to obtain a posterior distribution.
Bernoulli|A distribution with two outcomes, conventionally one with probability p and zero with probability 1−p.
Beta distribution|A probability distribution on [0,1] controlled by two positive shape parameters. It is conjugate to a Bernoulli likelihood.
Binomial|The distribution of the number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials with common success probability.
Boundary condition|A required value or relationship at the edge of a differential-equation domain.
Brownian motion|A continuous process starting at zero with independent normal increments of mean zero and variance equal to elapsed time.
Calibration|Choosing model parameters to fit specified observations or target quantities under a stated objective.
Capital|Financial resources held to absorb losses; a capital charge is different from the underlying exposure or valuation adjustment.
CDF|A cumulative distribution function gives the probability that a random variable is at or below a specified value.
Chain rule|The derivative rule for a composition: multiply the outer derivative, evaluated at the inner output, by the inner derivative.
Cholesky factorisation|Writing a positive-definite symmetric matrix as a lower triangular matrix times its transpose.
Compounding|Applying growth repeatedly to a balance that includes previously accumulated growth.
Concave|A function is concave when the line segment between any two graph points lies at or below the graph over a convex domain.
Conditional expectation|An average of a random variable given specified information; it can itself be a random variable before that information is observed.
Conditional probability|Probability within the reduced set of outcomes consistent with an observed event of positive probability.
Condition number|A measure of sensitivity to input errors; the spectral condition number of an invertible matrix is its largest singular value divided by its smallest.
Confidence interval|An interval generated by a procedure with a stated repeated-sampling coverage under its assumptions.
Conjugate prior|A prior family that yields a posterior in the same family after updating with a specified likelihood.
Continuity|At a point, nearby function values approach the function value at that point.
Convergence|Approach to a limiting value, function or distribution in a specified mathematical sense.
Convex|A function is convex when its value at any weighted average of two inputs is no greater than the corresponding weighted average of outputs, on a convex domain.
Correlation|Covariance divided by the product of positive standard deviations. It measures linear association, not every form of dependence.
Covariance|The expected product of two variables’ deviations from their respective means, when the required moments exist.
Curvature|How slope changes locally. In these calculus lessons it refers to second-derivative behaviour, not a specific regulatory curvature charge.
Default|Failure to meet a defined contractual obligation; its precise definition depends on the application.
Delta|A derivative of an instrument’s value with respect to the underlying price under a stated pricing model.
Density|Probability per unit of input. Integrating it over an interval gives probability for a continuous distribution.
Derivative|The limit of a function’s change divided by its input change, when that limit exists.
Determinant|A scalar associated with a square matrix that gives signed volume scaling and is zero for a singular matrix.
Diffusion|Random spreading or its associated smoothing equation; context determines whether the term refers to a process or a differential operator.
Discount factor|The present value of one unit paid at a specified future date under a stated discounting convention.
Domain|The allowed inputs of a function.
Drift|The coefficient of predictable time change in the specified stochastic model.
Eigenvalue|The factor by which a matrix scales an associated nonzero eigenvector.
Eigenvector|A nonzero vector whose direction is preserved up to sign by a matrix transformation.
Euler method|A first-order numerical method that advances a differential equation using its current slope.
Expected shortfall|The average of upper loss quantiles beyond a confidence level; for a continuous distribution it is the conditional mean beyond VaR.
Expectation|A probability-weighted average, defined by a sum or integral when the relevant integrability conditions hold.
Exponential|The function e^x, whose derivative equals itself. It describes constant proportional growth.
Factorial|For a positive integer n, n! is the product of integers from one to n; 0! is defined as one.
Filtration|An increasing family of information sets, formally sigma-algebras, indexed by time.
Finite difference|An approximation to a derivative using values at nearby inputs separated by a finite step.
Gamma|The second derivative of an instrument’s value with respect to the underlying price under a stated model.
Gradient|The vector of a scalar function’s partial derivatives.
Hedge|A position intended to offset a specified risk. It need not remove every risk or work equally for all shock sizes.
Hessian|The matrix of second partial derivatives of a scalar function.
IID|Independent and identically distributed. Each variable has the same distribution and the joint probabilities factorise across variables.
Independent|Knowing the outcome of one variable does not change the distribution of another; formally, joint probabilities factorise.
Initial condition|The starting value required to specify a dynamic equation’s solution.
Integral|A limit of accumulated contributions; definite integration can measure signed area, mass or other accumulated quantities.
Integrable|For expectations here, a variable is integrable when the expectation of its absolute value is finite.
Intensity|The expected event rate per unit time in a specified arrival model.
Interpolation|Estimating values between known input points according to a stated rule.
Itô integral|A stochastic integral defined from nonanticipating sums under suitable conditions. Its rules differ from integration along smooth paths.
Jacobian|The matrix of first partial derivatives of a vector-valued transformation.
Jensen’s inequality|A convex function of an expectation is no greater than the expectation of that function, when the quantities are defined.
Lagrange multiplier|An auxiliary variable used to express first-order conditions for a constrained optimum; it can measure local constraint sensitivity under regularity conditions.
Likelihood|The data probability or density viewed as a function of a model parameter, with the observed data fixed.
Limit|The value approached as an input, index or other quantity approaches a specified point or boundary.
Linear combination|A sum of vectors or quantities multiplied by scalar coefficients.
Logarithm|The inverse of exponentiation. The natural logarithm ln uses base e and is defined for positive real inputs.
Lognormal|A positive distribution whose natural logarithm is normally distributed.
Marginal distribution|The distribution of one variable obtained by summing or integrating a joint distribution over the other variables.
Martingale|An adapted integrable process whose conditional expected future value equals its present value under the specified measure and filtration.
Matrix|A rectangular array representing a linear map or a collection of coefficients.
Mean|The expectation of a distribution, or the arithmetic average of a sample, depending on context.
Mean reversion|A tendency in a model for deviations from a long-run level to pull back towards that level.
Median|A central quantile with at least half the probability at or below it and at least half at or above it.
Mixed partial|A second derivative taken with respect to two different inputs.
Moment|An expectation of a power of a variable, such as a mean or second moment, when finite.
Monte Carlo|Estimation of an expectation by averaging simulated samples.
Normal distribution|A symmetric continuous distribution specified by its mean and positive standard deviation.
Notional|A reference amount used to calculate contractual payments; it need not equal market value or loss exposure.
Null hypothesis|The reference statistical claim under which a test statistic’s distribution is calculated.
Null space|The set of input vectors mapped to zero by a linear transformation.
Numeraire|A strictly positive traded asset used as the unit in which other asset values are expressed in a pricing framework.
Objective|The quantity an optimisation problem seeks to minimise or maximise.
Orthogonal|Perpendicular in Euclidean geometry, or having zero inner product in a specified inner-product space.
Overfitting|Fitting sample-specific noise or quirks in a way that harms generalisation beyond the training data.
P-value|Under the null model, the probability of a test statistic at least as extreme as the observed statistic, according to the chosen test.
Partial derivative|A derivative with respect to one input while holding the others fixed.
Payoff|The contractual value or payment at a specified future outcome or date, before any required discounting.
PCA|Principal component analysis: an orthogonal decomposition ordering directions by explained variance in a centred dataset or covariance matrix.
Positive definite|A symmetric matrix is positive definite when its quadratic form is strictly positive for every nonzero vector.
Positive semidefinite|A symmetric matrix is positive semidefinite when its quadratic form is nonnegative for every vector.
Posterior|The distribution of a parameter after combining a prior and a likelihood and normalising.
Prior|A distribution representing parameter uncertainty before the observations used in an update.
Projection|The component of a vector lying in a specified direction or subspace under a chosen inner product.
Quadratic form|A scalar expression xᵀAx; covariance matrices use it to calculate portfolio variance.
Quadratic variation|The limit, in the relevant sense, of sums of squared increments over increasingly fine partitions.
Quantile|The smallest threshold at which a cumulative distribution reaches a specified probability level.
Rank|The dimension of a matrix’s column space, equal to the number of independent directions it spans.
Regression|A model or fitting method describing how an outcome varies with explanatory inputs; association alone does not establish causation.
Regularisation|A penalty or restriction introduced to stabilise fitting or control model complexity.
Replication|Constructing a portfolio whose payoff matches a claim in every state considered by the model.
Residual|The difference between an observed value and its fitted prediction.
Risk-neutral measure|A pricing probability measure under which appropriately discounted traded asset prices are martingales under the model’s assumptions.
Root|An input where a function equals zero.
Scalar|A single numerical coefficient, in contrast to a vector or matrix.
Secant|A line joining two points on a graph; its slope is an average rate of change.
Seed|An initial state used to reproduce a pseudorandom sequence. Reproducibility does not remove sampling error.
Sigma-algebra|A collection of events closed under complements and countable unions, specifying which events are measurable.
Singular|A square matrix is singular when it has no inverse, equivalently when its determinant is zero.
Singular value|A nonnegative stretch factor in the singular value decomposition of a matrix.
Span|The set of all linear combinations of a collection of vectors.
Standard deviation|The positive square root of variance, in the original variable’s units.
Standard error|The standard deviation of an estimator’s sampling distribution, which differs from individual-observation spread.
Stationarity|Stability of a process’s distributional characteristics over time; weak stationarity specifies stable first and second moments.
Stochastic|Involving randomness described by a probability model.
Strike|The contractual exercise price of an option.
Tangent|A line matching a differentiable graph’s value and local slope at a point.
Taylor expansion|A local approximation built from a function’s derivatives at a reference point, with a remainder that must be controlled.
Transpose|The matrix formed by interchanging rows and columns, denoted by a superscript T.
Truncation error|Error from cutting off an infinite process or replacing a limit with a finite approximation.
Variance|The expected squared deviation from a mean, if finite.
Vector|An ordered collection of quantities supporting addition and scalar multiplication.
Volatility|A standard deviation or diffusion scale defined for a particular quantity and time convention.
Weight|A coefficient determining a quantity’s contribution to a sum; portfolio weights may be constrained or signed.
Bootstrap|Resampling from an observed sample, usually with replacement, to approximate the sampling distribution of an estimator under suitable assumptions.
Characteristic function|The expectation of exp(iuX) as a function of frequency u. It always exists and uniquely determines a probability distribution.
Complementary slackness|A condition in constrained optimisation requiring each inequality multiplier times its constraint value to be zero at a KKT solution.
Complex number|A number with real and imaginary components, written a+ib where i²=−1.
Copula|A joint cumulative distribution with uniform margins that describes how marginal distributions are joined by dependence.
Empirical distribution|The distribution that places equal probability on each observed sample point, including repeated values.
Fourier series|A representation of a periodic function as a sum of sinusoidal components under specified convergence conditions.
Harmonic|A sinusoidal component with frequency equal to an integer multiple of a fundamental frequency.
Imaginary unit|The number i satisfying i²=−1, represented geometrically by a quarter-turn rotation under multiplication.
Innovation|In state estimation, the difference between a new observation and its predicted value.
Kalman gain|The coefficient controlling how a state estimate adjusts to an observation’s innovation, based on predicted and measurement uncertainty.
KKT conditions|Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions combine stationarity, primal feasibility, dual feasibility and complementary slackness for constrained optimisation, with appropriate assumptions.
Moment-generating function|The expectation of exp(tX) where finite. It may not exist on any open interval around zero.
Pareto distribution|A power-law distribution with survival probability (minimum/x)^α above a positive minimum, with moment existence determined by its positive shape α.
Percentile|A quantile whose probability level is expressed as a percentage.
Process noise|Uncertainty introduced as a state evolves between observations, separate from measurement error.
Tail dependence|The limiting tendency for one variable to be extreme given that another is extreme, defined separately for upper or lower tails.
Uniform distribution|A distribution assigning equal probability density across its interval; probabilities are proportional to interval length.
